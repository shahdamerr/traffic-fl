\chapter{Background and Literature Review}
\label{ch:background}

This chapter reviews the theoretical foundations of traffic flow prediction (TFP) and explores the evolution of forecasting methodologies from traditional statistical models to advanced deep learning architectures. It subsequently examines the paradigm shift toward Federated Learning (FL) within Intelligent Transportation Systems (ITS) to address privacy and communication bottlenecks. The review draws upon seminal works and recent advancements, synthesizing key methodological categories in FL—including communication efficiency, data heterogeneity, and security—to contextualize the contributions of this thesis.

\section{Traditional and Deep Learning Approaches to Traffic Flow Prediction}
Traffic flow prediction has been a cornerstone of intelligent transportation planning for decades. Early research predominantly relied on centralized machine learning and statistical methods, such as combining Principal Component Analysis (PCA) with Support Vector Regression (SVR) \cite{jin2008}, or utilizing fuzzy C-mean neural networks \cite{tang2017}. While these models laid the groundwork for data-driven management, they struggled to accurately capture the complex, non-linear spatiotemporal dependencies inherent in modern urban traffic networks.

With the proliferation of artificial intelligence, deep learning became the dominant approach. Spatial features from grid-based city structures were extracted using Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) \cite{zhang2017}, while sequence models like Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) \cite{hochreiter1997} and Gated Recurrent Units (GRU) \cite{cho2014} captured long-term temporal dependencies in congested roadways. To exploit both domains simultaneously, researchers introduced Spatio-Temporal Graph Neural Networks (STGNN). Methods such as the Diffusion Convolutional Recurrent Neural Network (DCRNN) \cite{li2018} modeled traffic flow as a diffusion process on a directed graph.

\section{Federated Learning in Intelligent Transportation Systems}
Despite the success of deep learning, centralized architectures require continuous uploading of raw sensor data to a cloud server, underutilizing edge computing and presenting significant privacy risks. Federated Learning (FL), introduced by McMahan et al. \cite{mcmahan2017}, mitigates these issues by allowing edge devices to collaboratively train a shared model without exchanging raw data. Recent surveys, such as those by Chong et al. \cite{chong2025} and Kairouz et al. \cite{kairouz2021}, highlight FL's critical role in mission-critical ITS tasks, while also identifying core challenges: statistical heterogeneity, communication overhead, and system security.

\subsection{Data Heterogeneity and Personalization}
A core challenge in FL is statistical heterogeneity, where data across clients is non-Independent and Identically Distributed (non-IID). Traffic patterns vary drastically between highway sensors and residential streets. To counter client-drift caused by non-IID data, Li et al. proposed FedProx \cite{li2020fedprox}, which adds a proximal term to keep local models close to the global model. Karimireddy et al. introduced SCAFFOLD \cite{karimireddy2020}, utilizing control variates to correct for drift, while MOON \cite{li2021moon} applied model contrastive learning to align representations. 

More dynamic approaches have also emerged. Cha and Chang \cite{cha2025} dynamically adjusted regularization strengths based on estimated data distributions, significantly improving accuracy in severely skewed scenarios. In traffic-specific applications, Huang et al. \cite{huang2024} utilized fairness-aware aggregation weights to reduce bias against under-represented edge-case vehicle scenarios, cutting edge-case prediction errors by nearly 29\%.

\subsection{Communication-Efficient and Scalable Aggregation}
Transmitting large deep learning models like Sequence-to-Sequence (Seq2Seq) GRUs over vehicular networks demands high bandwidth. To reduce this overhead, intelligent client selection strategies have been developed. The Oort framework \cite{lai2021oort} prioritizes clients based on data utility and system speed, while Albelaihi \cite{albelaihi2025} proposed MACS, a mobility-aware selection strategy that optimizes participation under latency constraints in IoT environments.

Model and structural optimizations further enhance scalability. Li et al. developed FedSTDN \cite{li2025fedstdn}, which utilized spatial-temporal clustering and data augmentation to group cellular traffic data, reducing uplink overhead while achieving a ~32.8\% MSE improvement. Architectural shifts, such as multi-server frameworks \cite{mazloomi2025}, alleviate single-server bottlenecks by performing edge-based local aggregation, reducing latency for highly mobile vehicular clients.

\subsection{Privacy and Security-Enhanced FL}
While FL intrinsically protects raw data, it remains vulnerable to model poisoning and single-point-of-failure vulnerabilities. Privacy can be further enhanced using Differential Privacy (DP), pioneered by Geyer et al. \cite{geyer2017}, which adds calibrated noise to updates. In highly sensitive V2V communications, Tahir et al. proposed FLAA-V2V \cite{tahir2025}, combining Local DP with Lightweight Homomorphic Encryption to maintain robust collision-avoidance capabilities even under targeted attacks.

Blockchain technology has increasingly been integrated to decentralize trust. Lu et al. \cite{lu2020} demonstrated asynchronous FL over a blockchain ledger in IoV, while Li et al. \cite{li2025dtbfl} proposed DTB-FL, utilizing digital twins and smart-contract-based reputation weighting to maintain high accuracy despite a large presence of malicious nodes. In the specific domain of traffic flow prediction, Zhu et al. \cite{zhu2025cmba} introduced the Communication-Mitigated and Blockchain-Assisted Federated Learning (CMBA-FL) framework. CMBA-FL pairs an Encoder-Decoder Seq2Seq architecture with a parameter relevance detector to drastically cut communication costs, relying on blockchain consensus to bypass central server vulnerabilities.

\section{Bridging to the Thesis Framework}
The literature demonstrates that while advanced local models (like Seq2Seq) achieve high accuracy, they require robust mechanisms to handle non-IID distributions and communication limits. Building upon the foundational Seq2Seq GRU architecture validated by CMBA-FL \cite{zhu2025cmba}, this thesis eschews the high complexity of blockchain infrastructure in favor of a novel \textit{hierarchical clustering approach}. By grouping sensors with similar temporal behaviors via Dynamic Time Warping \cite{feng2024}, and applying Top-K sparsification \cite{gao2024fedce} alongside quality-weighted aggregation \cite{dai2024}, this framework directly addresses the data heterogeneity and communication challenges identified in the literature, providing a scalable and efficient solution for federated traffic forecasting.
